Recap: One Job, Done Simply

// Structured Field HTTP response header: Connection-Allowlist: (response-origin "https://*.site.example" "https://cdn.example"); report-to=endpoint
  • Why not CSP? CSP mixes XSS, framing, and subresource rules in a complex grammar
  • One job: Block any outbound connection not matching the allowlist (URLPatterns + response-origin)
  • Covers Fetch, navigations, WebSockets, WebTransport, WebRTC, dns-prefetch, preconnect

Sometimes the best way to design a security primitive is to do one thing clearly. Content Security Policy tries to solve content injection, framing, and network restrictions all at once. Connection Allowlists focuses purely on exfiltration defense-in-depth: a single Structured Field header using URLPattern syntax that blocks unauthorized connections before they are made across Fetch, navigations, WebRTC, WebTransport, and preconnect/dns-prefetch.

Why Embedded Enforcement?

  • Cross-origin iframes bring their own policy container
  • Your exfiltration defense is only as strong as your most permissive child frame

A document's Connection-Allowlist only governs its own connections. But real pages embed cross-origin widgets, players, and subframes. Because a cross-origin iframe gets its own policy container from its own response, data the parent can't exfiltrate directly could be handed to a permissive child frame. Embedded enforcement lets the embedder extend its guarantee across the entire embedded subtree.

How Embedded Enforcement Works

<iframe connectionAllowlist='("https://api.example" response-origin)' src="https://widget.example/"></iframe>
  • Request: Sends Sec-Required-Connection-Allowlist header
  • Mandatory Opt-In: Child must assert a subset Connection-Allowlist, return Allow-Connection-Allowlist-From, or use a local scheme (srcdoc / data:)
  • No Escape by Nesting: Inherited by subframes & workers (can only tighten)

Modeled directly on CSP Embedded Enforcement: the embedder sets connectionAllowlist on the iframe, the browser sends Sec-Required-Connection-Allowlist, and the child must opt in either by already serving a subset Connection-Allowlist or by returning Allow-Connection-Allowlist-From. The unresolved requirement is inherited down the frame tree, and response-origin re-resolves to each frame's own origin.

Path to W3C & Upstreaming

  • 1. Graduate from WICG → W3C WebAppSec WG
  • 2. Upstream Fetch monkey-patches: Main Fetch + pass environment into resolve an origin & obtain a connection (#32)
  • 3. Upstream HTML monkey-patches: Policy container fields, <iframe connectionAllowlist>, dns-prefetch & preconnect
  • 4. WebRTC & Service Workers: Candidate check + navigate() / openWindow()

Our goal is to move Connection Allowlists from WICG into the W3C WebAppSec Working Group and upstream the monkey-patches into Fetch, HTML, WebRTC, and Service Workers. In particular, Fetch's low-level "resolve an origin" and "obtain a connection" algorithms need an environment parameter so lower-level connections like WebTransport, preconnect, and dns-prefetch are centrally checked.
